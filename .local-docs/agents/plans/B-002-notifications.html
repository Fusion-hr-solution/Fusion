Where Fusion stands today

Performance raises 29 kinds

Plans, reviews, calibration, 1:1s, feedback, praise and review reminders. Each one is written to an outbox and an in-app record in the same transaction, then emailed with up to 8 retries.

One bell for every module

The shell merges each module's feed through fusion-attention and polls every 60 seconds and on focus. Home shows work and upcoming dates, not news.

Email choices per person

Four groups can be switched off in Settings › Notifications. Deadline reminders are always emailed. The bell is always on.

English only

Titles are written in English when the notification is created and stored as sentences, so a French or Arabic reader still sees English.

Reminders on the UTC day

The hourly scan sends on the UTC date, around 01:00 in Tunis, weekends included. The company's time zone is already stored in Identity and not used.

Plain-text email

No layout, no "why you got this", no unsubscribe link or header. Identity's invitations already use an HTML layout.

Core HR and Identity are silent

A new manager, a joiner, a leaver or a new administrator role tells nobody. Core HR only serves four setup and access work items.

Kept 180 days

A daily purge removes older notifications. Read state is on the server, so it follows the person across devices.

The calls

Made from the code, first principles, and how Lattice, 15Five, HiBob and BambooHR handle it. Two are yours.

N1

Each module raises its own notifications. The plumbing becomes shared.

Outbox, in-app record, email layout, language and the unsubscribe link move from Performance into SharedKernel. Core HR and Identity use that kit. No central notification service.

Why: who receives a notification and what they may see are domain rules. A central service would have to copy every module's authorization or trust whatever it is told. The shell already merges the feeds, so the person sees one bell either way, and a new service would add another database and network hop on a machine that is already short of memory.

N2

Three kinds of message, three rules

KindExampleWhereCan be turned off
WorkReview Nour's planHome and sidebar counts, until done; a notification when it landsNo, it is the job
NewsHana gave you feedbackBell (live), and email per groupEmail yes, bell no
Account mailInvitation, administrator role grantedEmail onlyNever

Why: this is how Fusion already splits work items from notifications, and it matches Lattice, where tool notices can't be disabled and some emails carry no unsubscribe. Account mail is about access and security, so a preference must never silence it.

N3

Two channels: the bell and email

The bell is the record and stays on. Email is the only switchable channel. No Slack, browser push or SMS. Microsoft Teams is the first channel to add when a client asks.

Why: EY's clients run Microsoft 365, so Teams is the likely ask. Lattice and 15Five show per-person columns per channel; a second column is cheap to add later once the groups and rules below exist.

N4

Reminders at 09:00 company time, on workdays

News goes out as it happens (the outbox runs every 15 seconds). Reminders keep their schedule (3 days before, the day after, then weekly) but are sent at 09:00 in the company's time zone, Monday to Friday. No quiet hours for news.

Why: a reminder is only useful when someone can act on it, and a 01:00 email lands under a night's worth of mail. The time zone already lives on the company in Identity. News email isn't a phone buzz, so quiet hours would only delay it.

N5

Stored as codes and values, written in the reader's language

A notification stores what happened (a code, the names and dates), not a sentence. The module writes the sentence when it is read, in the reader's current language, so the bell contract stays as it is. Email is written at send time in the recipient's account language (their choice, then the company's, then English).

Why: the shell keeps rendering any module without knowing its kinds, which was the point of the contract. A reader who switches to French sees old notifications in French too. Names stay as people wrote them. This takes the email and notification parts of B-116 into this work; error codes and change history stay there.

N6

One branded email layout

HTML with a plain-text part, shared with Identity's invitations. Each email says why it was sent and links to Settings › Notifications. Switchable groups carry a one-click unsubscribe header that turns off that group only. Emails never quote answers, comments or ratings.

Why: Gmail and Outlook expect one-click unsubscribe from senders of regular mail. Keeping content out of email is already the Performance rule, because email leaves the company's control. Company logo and colours come with B-110.

N7

Company defaults per group (B-106)

For each email group a company picks one of three: Required (always emailed), On (people may turn it off) or Off (people may turn it on). New companies start where Fusion is today: deadline reminders required, the rest on. The bell is never affected. It lives in Settings › Company › Notifications for whoever administers that module.

Why: this is Lattice's model in fewer words, and 15Five greys out what the company locked. Groups, not the 29 kinds: nobody tunes 29 switches, and the groups are already what people see.

N8

Core HR and Identity start talking

Core HR: a person hears when their manager changes or a change to their job takes effect; a manager hears when someone joins or leaves their team. One new group, "Team and job changes", on by default. Identity sends account mail when someone gains or loses an administrator role.

Why: these are the moments people otherwise find out about by accident. A change recorded in advance notifies when it is recorded and appears under Upcoming on its date, so nobody is told twice.

N9

The bell rings live, through a content-free nudge

Each module keeps one open stream per signed-in person that only says "your feed changed". The shell then refetches the bell and Home through the normal endpoints. PostgreSQL LISTEN/NOTIFY carries the signal on commit; the gateway streams it with a heartbeat; polling stays as a slow fallback.

Why: the notification already exists the moment the action commits; only the bell's 60-second poll made people wait. In a demo with two windows side by side, even refetch-on-focus doesn't fire, because TanStack Query v5 refetches when a tab becomes visible, not when you click into another window. The stream carries no content, so it needs no new authorization, and PostgreSQL already works across instances. About a day's work.

N10

Read state and retention stay

Opening a notification marks it read; "Mark all read" stays; one unread count across modules; kept 180 days. Finishing the work doesn't touch the notification, because the work item already disappears.

N11

Colleagues' modules stay as they are

Learning, Recruitment, Interview and Onboarding keep their own emails. Any of them can join the bell later by serving the same three endpoints and adding one line to the module list.

N12

The recipient's language travels with their email address

When a module looks up where to send an email, it also gets the account language from Identity. No module stores a copy.

Why: the language belongs to the account (decided in B-105), and a copy would go stale the moment someone switches.

T1

One email per event, for now

Decided: one email per event; a "once a day" option later if managers say it's too much.

A manager with eight reports gets eight "self-assessment submitted" emails in a review week. Individual emails are simpler and each links straight to the work; a summary is calmer but delays everything to the next morning. Lattice and 15Five send per event.

T2

Four Core HR moments

Decided: the four in N8. Candidates I left out: a new hire's first day (to the whole team), a work anniversary (B-146 covers it on Home), a change to someone's job title (to their manager).

Each extra moment is a small backend change; the question is how chatty Fusion should feel.
